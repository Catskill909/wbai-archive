Listening

Counters only — there is no event log, no cookie, no session and no stored or hashed IP, so nothing links two events to the same person. That means unique listeners is not a number this app can produce, by choice. Searches are counted; the words typed never leave the browser.

Reach

Page views grouped by the timezone the browser reports — never by IP address, which is still never read, resolved or stored. A timezone is not a location: almost every browser in the eastern US reports America/New_York whether it sits in Brooklyn or Miami, so the first row is a clock, not a city. Read it as one question only — does this station reach past its own signal — and at no finer resolution than that. Travellers and VPNs count wherever their clock is set.

Thinnest coverage

The 12 shows with the least audio in the window. Deliberately the bottom of the ranking, not the top: upstream caps every feed at five episodes and — of — shows sit at that cap, so the top is a flat tie and says nothing. A show with a single episode has either just launched, airs rarely, or has a feed that stopped publishing — and only the last is a problem.

Coverage

How complete the data behind each feed is. These are three separate ratios, not a funnel: the program directory is keyed by title and the feeds by archive slug, so a show can be missing from one and present in the other. The title match is approximate for that reason.

Episodes by air date

Every day in the window, including the empty ones — the gaps are the point. Each show holds only its five most recent episodes, so a daily show covers five days and a monthly one covers five months. That is why the right-hand side is dense and the left is sparse and broken: what survives out there is a handful of infrequent shows, not a full schedule.

Every feed

Every show, with its plays and time listened over the window selected in the Listening section above — sortable and searchable, so a single show's figures are one filter away rather than only visible if it reaches the top twelve. A play is a click; the listened column is whether anyone stayed. Click a row for that show's whole recorded history, month by month.

Actions

The only controls here that change anything. Each one refreshes one of our caches from WBAI — nothing writes to the station's systems, and running one twice is the same as running it once. Re-checking every feed is 122 requests to a small server, so it is rate limited.

Storage

Whether this deployment keeps its data across a redeploy. The only field that proves it is the instance id staying the same across two deploys.

Upstream

Timed from the requests the app already makes — no synthetic pings. WBAI runs a small server, and monitoring it by adding traffic to it would be self-defeating. A 404 is counted apart from a failure: 33 of the slugs the listing advertises have no feed behind them, so probing them 404s by design.

Export data

Reports for Excel, Google Sheets or Pacifica, and a backup for moving this app and its archive to another server. Nothing in any of these files identifies a listener.

One page with everything for the dates you pick: listening at a glance, minutes per day, most listened shows, reach, what the archive aired, and the program data gaps to fix at WBAI’s archive. It opens in a new tab; use Print or save as PDF there.
