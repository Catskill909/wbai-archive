The 12 shows with the least audio in the window. Deliberately the bottom of the ranking, not the top: upstream caps every feed at five episodes and — of — shows sit at that cap, so the top is a flat tie and says nothing. Coverage

How complete the data behind each feed is. These are three separate ratios, not a funnel: the program directory is keyed by title and the feeds by archive slug, so a show can be missing from one and present in the other. The title match is approximate for that reason.

Episodes by air date

Every day in the window, including the empty ones — the gaps are the point. Each show holds only its five most recent episodes, so a daily show covers five days and a monthly one covers five months. That is why the right-hand side is dense and the left is sparse and broken: what survives out there is a handful of infrequent shows, not a full schedule.

Every feed

Every show, with its plays and time listened this month — sortable and searchable, so a single show's figures are one filter away rather than only visible if it reaches the top twelve. A play is a click; the listened column is whether anyone stayed.

Actions

The only controls here that change anything. Each one refreshes one of our caches from WBAI — nothing writes to the station's systems, and running one twice is the same as running it once. Re-checking every feed is 122 requests to a small server, so it is rate limited.

Storage

Whether this deployment keeps its data across a redeploy. The only field that proves it is the instance id staying the same across two deploys.
